\section{Research gaps}
Three gaps motivate our work. \emph{(i) Prediction circularity.} In a standard
CBM the concepts are predicted from the whole image, so a concept can be inferred
through global, class-correlated shortcuts instead of the evidence it
names~\cite{margeloiu2021concept}. The bottleneck then becomes circular and leaky:
the concept prediction is entangled with the class prediction, which undermines
the faithfulness of the explanation and the reliability of concept interventions.
Approaches that ground each concept on the specific region it describes---so that
the concept prediction cannot short-circuit through the class---remain
comparatively rare, and this is the primary motivation for our part-conditioned
design. \emph{(ii) Structure studied in isolation.} Structure in the concept space
(grounding, coarse-to-fine concepts) and in the label space (a taxonomy over
classes) is usually studied separately; combining them inside a single supervised
CBM is under-explored. \emph{(iii) Severity is not measured.} CBM evaluation
typically reports accuracy only, ignoring \emph{error severity}---precisely the
quantity a class taxonomy is expected to improve. We address these by grounding
concepts to break circularity, structuring both the concept and the label space,
and evaluating with an explicit severity metric while holding model capacity
fixed, so that observed differences are attributable to the bottleneck structure
rather than to the feature extractor.
