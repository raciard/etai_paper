\textbf{The interpretability bottleneck has a real cost.} The black-box beats
every concept-based variant on \emph{both} metrics---accuracy $85.8\%$ against
$74$--$79\%$, and severity $0.327$ against $0.52$--$0.78$---even though its
checkpoint was early-stopped (epoch $18/30$, so its converged numbers would likely
be higher still). This is the cost of interpretability documented by
\citet{koh2020concept}: routing the prediction through ${\sim}109$ hand-annotated
concepts is a genuine information bottleneck that forbids the visual shortcuts a
free network can exploit. The concept-based models pay accuracy and severity for
something the black-box cannot provide---an inspectable set of intermediate
concepts.

\textbf{The sequential classifier dominates the independent one.} Training the
species classifier on the concept stage's own predictions (sequential) rather than
on the ground-truth concept labels (independent) improves top-1 accuracy for every
variant---strongly for Flat ($73.9 \rightarrow 78.9$) and by a smaller margin for
the part-conditioned models ($74.3 \rightarrow 76.4$ and $73.3 \rightarrow
74.6$)---and lowers severity throughout. Learning on the real, noisy concept
probabilities lets the classifier absorb the concept stage's systematic errors,
whereas the independent classifier, fit on clean labels, is mismatched to the
predictions it faces at test time. We therefore read the effect of grounding off
the sequential column, where the models are strongest.

\textbf{Grounding concepts costs accuracy---as expected.} Within the sequential
classifier, predicting concepts from their own part lowers top-1 accuracy from
$78.9\%$ to $76.4\%$, and adding the coarse-to-fine structure lowers it further to
$74.6\%$; average severity rises in step ($0.52 \rightarrow 0.65 \rightarrow
0.72$). This is consistent with our motivation rather than a failure: the flat
model reaches higher accuracy partly by exploiting whole-image, class-correlated
shortcuts that part conditioning deliberately removes. The concept-space design
therefore \emph{trades} a little accuracy for a bottleneck that is faithful---each
concept grounded in the region it names, so its prediction is not circular---and
hierarchically organized for color and pattern, the intended interpretability
benefit that by construction is not visible in accuracy.

\textbf{CRM helps only the uncertain, grounded models.} The severity-aware rule
lowers severity on the part-conditioned model in \emph{both} regimes ($0.734
\rightarrow 0.727$ and $0.649 \rightarrow 0.641$) at an accuracy cost below $0.4$
points, but does nothing for Flat-independent ($0.658$) and slightly worsens the
sharper models---Flat-sequential ($0.523 \rightarrow 0.550$) and, most tellingly,
the highly accurate black-box ($0.327 \rightarrow 0.342$)---where there is little
error mass for a re-ranking to improve. The gain is therefore real but targeted,
and it depends on calibration: with the default temperature ($T{=}1$) CRM degrades
both metrics, and only after temperature scaling (here $T{\approx}1.35$) does it
behave as intended~\cite{karthik2021nocost}.
