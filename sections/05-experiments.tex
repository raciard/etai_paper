\textbf{The interpretability bottleneck has a measurable cost.} The black-box
outperforms every concept-based variant on \emph{both} metrics: accuracy reaches
$85.8\%$ versus $74$--$79\%$, and severity drops to $0.327$ versus
$0.52$--$0.78$. Note that the black-box checkpoint was early-stopped at epoch
$18/30$, so its converged performance would likely be higher still. This gap is
the cost of interpretability already documented by \citet{koh2020concept}:
routing the prediction through ${\sim}109$ hand-annotated concepts is a real
information bottleneck that prevents the network from exploiting visual shortcuts.
The concept-based models sacrifice accuracy and severity in exchange for
something the black-box cannot offer---an inspectable set of intermediate
concepts.

\textbf{Errors decompose into how often and how badly.} Table~\ref{tab:severity-breakdown}
splits the severity of Table~\ref{tab:results} into how often each model errs
and, conditioned on an error, how severe it is and where in the taxonomy it
lands. Under arg\,max, the black-box's errors are, on average, the mildest of
any variant (Sev.\ (err.)$=2.307$) and the most likely to stay within the true
genus ($29.3\%$, against $23.7$--$28.0\%$ for the concept-based models).
Grounding concepts on their part therefore does not only cost accuracy in
aggregate: conditioned on making a mistake, the part-conditioned and
hierarchical models' errors are also proportionally farther in the taxonomy
(Far share $65.2$--$66.3\%$, against $59.9\%$ for the black-box and
$62.0$--$63.6\%$ for Flat)---a cost the aggregate severity of
Table~\ref{tab:results} already reflects but that this decomposition makes
explicit, rather than leaving it implicit in a single averaged number. CRM
leaves this breakdown largely unchanged for the models it does not help
(black-box, Flat-independent), and for the part-conditioned and hierarchical
models it lowers Sev.\ (err.) by a modest, consistent margin ($0.04$--$0.08$)
while mostly redistributing errors within the Same-genus/Same-family/Far split
rather than moving them cleanly from Far into the closer buckets---consistent
with Table~\ref{tab:results}'s finding that CRM's severity gain there comes
from a few re-ranked borderline cases rather than a wholesale change in error
character.

\textbf{The sequential classifier consistently outperforms the independent one.}
Training the species classifier on the concept encoder's own predictions
(sequential) rather than on the ground-truth labels (independent) raises top-1
accuracy across the board: by about $5$ points for Flat ($73.9 \to 78.9$) and by
$2$--$1.3$ points for the part-conditioned variants ($74.3 \to 76.4$ and
$73.3 \to 74.6$). Severity decreases accordingly. The explanation is
straightforward: the sequential classifier is trained on the noisy concept
probabilities it will actually see at test time, so it can absorb the concept
encoder's systematic biases, whereas the independent classifier, fit on clean
labels, faces a train--test mismatch. Because the sequential regime gives each
variant its best performance, we read the effect of grounding from that column.

\textbf{Grounding concepts reduces accuracy---by design.} Within the sequential
regime, predicting concepts from their own body part lowers top-1 accuracy from
$78.9\%$ to $76.4\%$; adding the coarse-to-fine structure brings it down further
to $74.6\%$. Average severity rises in tandem ($0.52 \to 0.65 \to 0.72$). This
drop is expected rather than a shortcoming: the flat model reaches higher
accuracy partly because it can exploit whole-image, class-correlated shortcuts
that part conditioning deliberately removes. The concept-space design therefore
trades a few accuracy points for a bottleneck that is faithful---each concept is
grounded in the body region it names and cannot circularly infer the answer from
global cues---and that organises colour and pattern concepts hierarchically, an
interpretability benefit not visible in accuracy numbers.

\textbf{Higher concept scores do not imply more faithful concepts.}
Table~\ref{tab:concepts} evaluates concept quality in isolation. The flat
encoder achieves a macro-$F_1$ of $0.876$, well above the part-conditioned
encoder's $0.758$---even though the latter is the one that actually breaks
prediction circularity. Far from contradicting our thesis, this gap
\emph{supports} it: the flat encoder can infer an attribute from whole-image cues
that are diagnostic of the species (e.g.\ recognising \emph{wing color} from the
overall plumage pattern), the same shortcut mechanism that inflates its species
accuracy in Table~\ref{tab:results}. The part-conditioned encoder, forced to work
from the crop of the relevant body part, cannot rely on those shortcuts; its
lower score is therefore a more honest reflection of whether the concept is truly
grounded in its own evidence. Adding the coarse-to-fine gate barely changes
concept quality ($0.758 \to 0.753$): the extra structure does not come at a
further cost.

\textbf{CRM helps the uncertain, grounded models.} The severity-aware decision
rule lowers severity on the part-conditioned model in \emph{both} classifier
regimes ($0.734 \to 0.727$, $0.649 \to 0.641$) at less than $0.4$ points of
accuracy. For the sharper, more confident models, however, CRM has little room
to improve: it leaves Flat-independent unchanged ($0.658$) and slightly worsens
Flat-sequential ($0.523 \to 0.550$) and the black-box ($0.327 \to 0.342$), where
there is almost no error mass for a re-ranking to act on. The benefit is
therefore real but selective, and it depends on calibration: at the default
temperature ($T{=}1$) CRM degrades both metrics; only after temperature scaling
(here $T \approx 1.35$) does it behave as intended~\cite{karthik2021nocost}.
