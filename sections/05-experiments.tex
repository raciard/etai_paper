\section{Experiments and analysis}
\label{sec:experiments}
We compare accuracy and severity across all variants, check whether part
conditioning is actually grounded rather than relying on shortcuts, and
evaluate CRM, a severity-aware decision rule.

\subsection{Accuracy and severity}
Table~\ref{tab:results} reports top-1 accuracy and mistake severity for every
variant. As expected, the black-box beats every concept-based variant. This is the known cost of forcing predictions
through a concept bottleneck~\cite{koh2020concept}. Within the
concept-based variants, accuracy falls step by step as we add structure---
flat, then part-conditioned, then $+$hierarchical---with severity rising in
tandem. This is expected: the flat model's edge comes partly from
whole-image shortcuts that part conditioning removes (Section~\ref{sec:concept-quality}).

\subsection{Concept quality}
\label{sec:concept-quality}
Table~\ref{tab:concepts} scores each concept encoder alone (binary accuracy,
macro-$F_1$), independent of the species classifier. Flat scores higher than
part-conditioned: it can infer an attribute from whole-image cues instead of
the region it names, the same shortcut that inflates its species accuracy
(Table~\ref{tab:results}). Part-conditioned only sees the relevant crop, so
its score reflects real grounding. The coarse gate itself is accurate
($96.3\%$ macro accuracy, $0.917$ macro-$F_1$ over the $22$ coarse-mapped
groups); errors concentrate in the fine value, not the coarse family.

\subsection{Concept consistency test}
Table~\ref{tab:concepts} only shows that the flat encoder's score is
\emph{consistent} with a shortcut; it does not prove it. To test grounding
directly, we take $100$ CUB-200-2011 test images with an unambiguous crown
colour, edit \emph{only} that colour with a generative image model (Figure~\ref{fig:crown-edit-example}), and re-run each
concept encoder on the edited image. 
A grounded encoder should change its
crown-colour prediction, since the crop it actually looks at has changed; an
encoder relying on whole-image shortcuts would probably not change most of its predictions.
Table~\ref{tab:crown-intervention} reports, per encoder, how often the
prediction changes at all after the intervention, and how often it lands on the exact colour we
edited towards.

The flat encoder barely reacts to the edit ($25\%$): it appears to lean on
its correlation with the rest of the image more than on the crown itself.
Both part-conditioned encoders, which can only see the crown crop, do not
have that option and change their prediction on about two-thirds of edits
instead.

\subsection{Severity-aware decision (CRM)}
CRM~\cite{karthik2021nocost} re-ranks an already-trained classifier's
posterior by expected severity instead of taking the arg\,max, with no
retraining and no extra parameters. Applied exactly as
\citet{karthik2021nocost} define it, it lowers severity for five of the six
concept-based rows in Table~\ref{tab:results}, usually at a small accuracy
cost or none at all; for $+$hierarchical Sequential it raises accuracy by
$1.5$ points while lowering severity too. The exception is Flat Sequential,
whose posterior is already sharp and confident, leaving CRM nothing useful
to re-rank, so severity goes up instead. CRM's benefit is real but
conditional on the classifier it is layered onto.
