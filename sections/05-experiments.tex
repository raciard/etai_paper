\textbf{The interpretability bottleneck has a measurable cost.} The black-box
outperforms every concept-based variant on \emph{both} metrics: accuracy reaches
$85.8\%$ versus $72$--$79\%$, and severity drops to $0.326$ versus
$0.53$--$0.82$. Note that the black-box checkpoint was early-stopped at epoch
$18/30$, so its converged performance would likely be higher still. This gap is
the cost of interpretability already documented by \citet{koh2020concept}:
routing the prediction through ${\sim}109$ hand-annotated concepts is a real
information bottleneck that prevents the network from exploiting visual shortcuts.
The concept-based models sacrifice accuracy and severity in exchange for
something the black-box cannot offer---an inspectable set of intermediate
concepts.

\textbf{Errors decompose into how often and how badly.} Table~\ref{tab:severity-breakdown}
splits the severity of Table~\ref{tab:results} into how often each model errs
and, conditioned on an error, how severe it is and where in the taxonomy it
lands. Under arg\,max, the black-box's errors are, on average, the mildest of
any variant (Sev.\ (err.)$=2.303$) and the most likely to stay within the true
genus ($29.4\%$, against $22.7$--$27.6\%$ for the concept-based models).
Grounding concepts on their part therefore does not only cost accuracy in
aggregate: conditioned on making a mistake, the part-conditioned and
hierarchical models' errors are also proportionally farther in the taxonomy
(Far share $65.2$--$67.8\%$, against $59.8\%$ for the black-box and
$62.6$--$62.7\%$ for Flat)---a cost the aggregate severity of
Table~\ref{tab:results} already reflects but that this decomposition makes
explicit, rather than leaving it implicit in a single averaged number. CRM's
effect on this breakdown is uneven rather than uniform across variants; we
discuss why alongside the accuracy/severity numbers below.

\textbf{The sequential classifier usually outperforms the independent one.}
Training the species classifier on the concept encoder's own predictions
(sequential) rather than on the ground-truth labels (independent) raises top-1
accuracy for Flat (by $5.9$ points, $73.4 \to 79.2$) and, more modestly, for
Part-conditioned ($73.1 \to 73.7$); for $+$hierarchical concepts the two are
statistically indistinguishable ($72.3\%$ vs.\ $72.2\%$). Severity moves the
same way. The explanation for the general pattern is straightforward: the
sequential classifier is trained on the noisy concept probabilities it will
actually see at test time, so it can absorb the concept encoder's systematic
biases, whereas the independent classifier, fit on clean labels, faces a
train--test mismatch; adding the coarse-to-fine gate on top evidently leaves
little of that mismatch left to absorb. Because the sequential regime gives
each variant its best or tied-best performance, we read the effect of
grounding from that column.

\textbf{Grounding concepts reduces accuracy---by design.} Within the sequential
regime, predicting concepts from their own body part lowers top-1 accuracy from
$79.2\%$ to $73.7\%$; adding the coarse-to-fine structure brings it down further
to $72.2\%$. Average severity rises in tandem ($0.53 \to 0.76 \to 0.80$). This
drop is expected rather than a shortcoming: the flat model reaches higher
accuracy partly because it can exploit whole-image, class-correlated shortcuts
that part conditioning deliberately removes. The concept-space design therefore
trades a few accuracy points for a bottleneck that is faithful---each concept is
grounded in the body region it names and cannot circularly infer the answer from
global cues---and that organises colour and pattern concepts hierarchically, an
interpretability benefit not visible in accuracy numbers.

\textbf{Higher concept scores do not imply more faithful concepts.}
Table~\ref{tab:concepts} evaluates concept quality in isolation. The flat
encoder achieves a macro-$F_1$ of $0.876$, well above the part-conditioned
encoder's $0.758$---even though the latter is the one that actually breaks
prediction circularity. Far from contradicting our thesis, this gap
\emph{supports} it: the flat encoder can infer an attribute from whole-image cues
that are diagnostic of the species (e.g.\ recognising \emph{wing color} from the
overall plumage pattern), the same shortcut mechanism that inflates its species
accuracy in Table~\ref{tab:results}. The part-conditioned encoder, forced to work
from the crop of the relevant body part, cannot rely on those shortcuts; its
lower score is therefore a more honest reflection of whether the concept is truly
grounded in its own evidence. Adding the coarse-to-fine gate barely changes
concept quality ($0.758 \to 0.753$): the extra structure does not come at a
further cost.

\textbf{CRM works out of the box once the posterior is reliable.} Applied
literally---raw ($T{=}1$) softmax, no tuning, exactly as
\citet{karthik2021nocost} define it (Sec.~\ref{sec:methodology})---CRM lowers
severity for Flat-Independent ($0.682 \to 0.663$), both Part-conditioned
regimes ($0.795 \to 0.777$, $0.763 \to 0.741$), and
$+$hierarchical-Independent ($0.822 \to 0.814$), each at $\leq 0.4$ points of
accuracy. Getting here required diagnosing why an earlier version of this
table showed CRM making \emph{every} row worse: the Stage-3 linear species
head was trained with a small $\ell_2$ penalty against overfitting (its $62$k
parameters see only ${\sim}9.4$k training images), which shrank its logits
enough that the raw posterior became severely underconfident---mean top-1
probability $0.26$ at $74\%$ accuracy---without improving held-out accuracy at
all. Feeding CRM a posterior that unreliable is close to feeding it noise: its
expected-severity re-ranking is only as good as the probabilities it
re-ranks. Removing the penalty restores CRM's literal, hyperparameter-free
behaviour for five of the six concept-based rows in Table~\ref{tab:results}.
The exception is $+$hierarchical-Sequential, whose posterior stays moderately
underconfident even without weight decay (mean top-1 probability $0.38$ at
$72\%$ accuracy): literal CRM there degrades both accuracy ($72.2 \to 69.0$)
and severity ($0.80 \to 0.94$), the one row where Table~\ref{tab:severity-breakdown}'s
CRM columns move in the wrong direction. A temperature fit on the validation
split alone ($T \approx 0.55$) recovers the expected behaviour there (accuracy
$72.0$, severity $0.77$); we report this as an ablation rather than the
default, since \citet{karthik2021nocost}'s method assumes no such tuning
should be necessary. The black-box and Flat-Sequential are the remaining two
cases, and a different one: their raw posteriors are already sharp
(mean top-1 probability $0.85$ for the black-box), so CRM has little room to
improve and instead costs a small, consistent amount ($0.326 \to 0.330$,
$0.531 \to 0.544$).
