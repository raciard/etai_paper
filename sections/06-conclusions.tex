\section{Conclusions}
We introduced hierarchical structure into a supervised CBM along two
axes---part-grounded, coarse-to-fine concepts in the concept space, and a
WordNet taxonomy with an LCA severity metric plus a post-hoc CRM decision rule
in the label space---and evaluated all variants with a hierarchy-aware metric
while keeping the backbone fixed. Compared with an unconstrained black-box, the
concept bottleneck costs both accuracy and severity; this is the well-documented
price of interpretability and sets the frame for interpreting our results.
Within that frame, grounding each concept on its body part eliminates the
prediction circularity of a whole-image bottleneck and, together with the
concept hierarchy, strengthens faithfulness and interpretability without
improving---and marginally hurting---accuracy and severity. The label-space
decision rule, in turn, lowers mistake severity at test time at virtually no
accuracy cost on most variants, provided the classifier it is layered onto
gives it a reasonably faithful posterior to work with. These two forms of hierarchy thus
serve complementary purposes and work best together: structuring the concepts
improves interpretability, while structuring the label space yields
semantically closer errors. Promising directions for future work include
hierarchy-aware training losses, extending the coarse-to-fine scheme to
attribute groups beyond colour and pattern, and pairing CRM with a
hierarchically structured classifier head.
