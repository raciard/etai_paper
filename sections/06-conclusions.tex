\section{Conclusions}
We added structure to a supervised CBM along two axes---part-grounded,
coarse-to-fine concepts in the concept space, and a WordNet taxonomy with an LCA
severity metric and a post-hoc CRM decision in the label space---and evaluated
with a hierarchy-aware metric while holding capacity fixed. Against an
unconstrained black-box the concept bottleneck costs both accuracy and severity---%
the documented price of interpretability---which sets the frame for our results:
grounding each concept on its part breaks the prediction circularity of a
whole-image bottleneck and, together with the concept hierarchy, improves
faithfulness and interpretability without improving (and slightly costing)
accuracy and severity, whereas the calibrated label-space rule lowers mistake
severity for free at test time on the part-grounded models. The practical
takeaway is that these two forms of hierarchy serve different ends and are best
combined: structure the concepts for interpretability, and structure the label
space for better mistakes. Future work includes hierarchy-aware training losses,
extending the coarse-to-fine scheme beyond color and pattern, and pairing CRM
with a hierarchically-structured classifier head.
