\section{Methodology}
\textbf{Setup.} We work on CUB-200-2011~\cite{wah2011caltech} (200 species,
bounding-box crops). As concepts we use the class-level attributes obtained with
the procedure of \citet{koh2020concept}: the noisy instance-level CUB attributes
are aggregated to the class level by \emph{majority voting}---an attribute is set
for a class when it is present in the majority of that class's images---and
attributes that remain rare across classes are discarded, yielding the denoised
set of $109$ concepts we predict (directly supervised). All variants share the
same feature extractor---a ConvNeXt-Tiny~\cite{liu2022convnet} backbone trained
from scratch---so that any difference reflects the bottleneck structure and not
model capacity. A no-bottleneck black-box (a ConvNeXt-Tiny backbone with a linear
head mapping the image directly to the species) is kept as a conceptual reference
for the accuracy cost of imposing interpretability. A linear classifier maps the
concept vector to the 200 species in every concept-based variant.

\textbf{Flat CBM.} A whole-image ConvNeXt-Tiny encoder predicts all $109$ concepts
at once (sigmoid), with no part localization; the concept vector then feeds the
species classifier.

\textbf{Part-conditioned CBM: breaking prediction circularity.} To stop a concept
from being predicted through whole-image, class-correlated shortcuts, we localize
the evidence for each concept \emph{before} predicting it. A frozen Keypoint R-CNN
detector localizes 12 anatomical parts (beak, wings, tail, \dots) together with
their visibility, producing one crop per part plus a global crop of the bird. The
part crops and the global crop are encoded by ConvNeXt-Tiny backbones (one shared
across all parts, one dedicated to the global crop). Each attribute is then
predicted \emph{only} from the crop of the part it names: the head for \emph{wing
color} sees only the wing, so the concept must be grounded in
its own local evidence and cannot short-circuit through features that merely
correlate with the species. The concept prediction is thus decoupled from the
class prediction and the bottleneck is faithful rather than circular. Parts that
are not localized are replaced by a learned ``absent'' embedding. By construction
this grounding is expected to cost some accuracy, precisely because it removes the
whole-image shortcuts a flat model would otherwise exploit.

\textbf{Coarse-to-fine concepts.} On top of the grounded concepts we add hierarchy
\emph{within} the concept space: for the color/pattern attribute groups, the fine
value is not predicted directly---a small coarse head first estimates a coarse
family (e.g.\ warm / cool / neutral / iridescent), whose posterior is concatenated
to the part embedding to condition the fine head that predicts the specific value.
The hierarchy acts on the concepts, not on the final species output.

\textbf{Training the classifier: independent vs.\ sequential.} The linear species
classifier on top of the concept vector is trained in two regimes that we compare
throughout. In the \emph{independent} regime it is fit on the ground-truth concept
labels; in the \emph{sequential} regime it is fit on the concept probabilities
that the concept stage actually predicts, so that it can learn to compensate for
the concept predictor's systematic errors. Both are evaluated on the real concept
predictions, and the concept stage itself is identical in the two regimes.

\textbf{Label-space hierarchy and mistake severity.} The 200 species are the
leaves of a taxonomic tree $\mathcal{T}$ obtained by mapping each class onto
WordNet (species\,$\rightarrow$\,genus\,$\rightarrow$\,family\,$\rightarrow
\cdots$)~\cite{miller1995wordnet}. For two nodes $a,b$ let $\mathrm{LCA}(a,b)$ be
their lowest common ancestor in $\mathcal{T}$, and let the \emph{height} $h(v)$ of
a node $v$ be the number of edges on the longest path from $v$ down to a leaf of
its subtree, so that $h(\cdot)=0$ for a leaf. Following
\citet{bertinetto2020making}, the severity of predicting $\hat{y}$ for a
ground-truth class $y$ is the height of their LCA,
\begin{equation}
  s(y,\hat{y}) \;=\; h\!\left(\mathrm{LCA}(y,\hat{y})\right),
  \label{eq:cost}
\end{equation}
which is $0$ for a correct prediction, $1$ when the two species share the genus,
$2$ when they share only the family, and larger for more distant confusions. The
mistake severity we report is the mean of this cost over the $N$ test examples,
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{Sev} \;=\; \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N} s\!\left(y_i,\hat{y}_i\right)
  \;=\; \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N} h\!\left(\mathrm{LCA}(y_i,\hat{y}_i)\right),
  \label{eq:sev}
\end{equation}
so that correct predictions contribute nothing and the score captures jointly how
often the model errs and how far, in the taxonomy, its errors fall (lower is
better).

\textbf{Severity-aware decision (CRM).} The \emph{same} cost drives the decision
rule. Let $p(c\mid x)$ be the model's posterior over the $C{=}200$ classes,
obtained from the species logits through a softmax with temperature $T$ tuned on a
held-out validation set. Instead of the arg\,max, CRM~\cite{karthik2021nocost}
returns the class of minimum \emph{expected} severity,
\begin{equation}
  \hat{y}_{\mathrm{CRM}}
  \;=\; \arg\min_{k\in\{1,\dots,C\}} \sum_{c=1}^{C} s(k,c)\,p(c\mid x),
  \label{eq:crm}
\end{equation}
so that, when the model is uncertain, it prefers a class taxonomically close to
the likely candidates over a far one. CRM is fully post-hoc and requires no
retraining.

\textbf{Metrics.} At the species level we report top-1 accuracy and the mistake
severity of Eq.~\eqref{eq:sev} (lower is better), the latter capturing how
semantically close the errors are. At the concept level we separately report
binary accuracy and macro-averaged $F_1$ over the $109$ attributes for the
concept encoder alone, to check whether a numerically higher concept score
reflects genuinely grounded evidence rather than a class-correlated shortcut.
