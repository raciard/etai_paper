\section{Related work}
\textbf{Concept bottlenecks.} \citet{koh2020concept} introduced the
image$\rightarrow$concepts$\rightarrow$class factorisation together with the
concept-level interpretability and intervention it affords. Our concept layer
follows their supervised, class-level attribute setup. Later analyses have shown,
however, that concepts predicted from the full image are not always learned as
intended: they can encode (``leak'') class information that goes beyond the
evidence they are supposed to capture~\cite{margeloiu2021concept}. This
observation motivates predicting each concept from a localised region instead.

\textbf{Hierarchy in the concept space.}
\citet{pittino2023hierarchical} aggregate concepts hierarchically for
explainable fine-grained classification, showing that grouping structure over
concepts can be useful; \citet{sun2024boosting} add supervised hierarchical
concept learning with structural constraints between coarse and fine levels.
Hierarchical prototypes~\cite{hase2019interpretable} pursue interpretable
recognition through an explicit top-down decomposition. These works inform our
part-grounded and coarse-to-fine concept design.

\textbf{Hierarchy in the label space and better mistakes.}
\citet{bertinetto2020making} formalise mistake severity via the LCA of the
predicted and true class in a semantic hierarchy and propose hierarchy-aware
training losses. \citet{karthik2021nocost} demonstrate that severity can be
reduced purely at test time, without any retraining, by selecting the class of
minimum expected risk---a mechanism they call Conditional Risk Minimisation
(CRM), which we adopt for our label-space axis.

\textbf{Positioning.} The survey of \citet{poeta2025concept} identifies
hierarchical concept structure as an open direction in concept-based
explainability. Our two-axis study addresses this gap directly.
